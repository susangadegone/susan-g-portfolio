01

Participation without pressure.

The hardest part of a wellness program is getting people to come when they don't have to. Nobody in a workforce reports to the wellness team. Trauma-informed care taught me how to earn showing up instead of demanding it: make the first step small, be clear about what happens to someone's information, and never use guilt or fear to fill a room.

That's how I worked for five years on the floor of a youth drop-in center. It's how I'd run an employee wellbeing program.

I'm most useful to a team that wants wellness offerings people use, not ones they get reminded about.

03

Programs I've run.

What needed to happen, what I did, and what changed.

01 · Moving a drop-in center online
In-person services shut down. Youth who relied on the center for food, homework help and daily safety check-ins lost their place to go.
What I did: Worked out which parts of walking through the door actually mattered, then rebuilt those in virtual tools that weren't designed for it.
Result: The center kept running fully online, and people kept showing up.
02 · Outreach that grew participation 200%
The center needed to reach youth who weren't coming in on their own.
What I did: Built a pandemic-era outreach program, then started the center's first Instagram in 2022.
Result: Participation grew 200%. The account now has about 550 followers.
03 · Training new staff
A 10+ person team of clinicians, case managers and support staff, with new hires joining over time.
What I did: Trained new hires on trauma-informed care plans as they came on, and led those plans for youth and families.
