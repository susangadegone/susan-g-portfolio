Hi, I'm Susan

I build wellness programs people actually show up for.

01

Participation without pressure.

The hardest part of a wellness program is getting people to come when they don't have to. Nobody in a workforce reports to the wellness team. Trauma-informed care taught me how to earn showing up instead of demanding it: make the first step small, be clear about what happens to someone's information, and never use guilt or fear to fill a room.

That's how I worked for four years leading peer teams at two youth drop-in centers. It's how I'd run an employee wellbeing program, and how I judge the digital tools that go with it.

02

What four years of behavioral health program work looks like in a wellness role.

01 · Running programs
I owned the yearly events (Back to School, Hoodie Haul, Thanksgiving Dinner) plus other center events. When COVID shut down in-person services, I rebuilt them into delivery and online programming, and people kept showing up.
In a wellness program: Launching a campaign or challenge, and keeping it going after the kickoff email.
02 · Leading people
I supervised peer partners across two centers, handled hiring, scheduling and staffing decisions, set program priorities, and built the new-hire training program, which cut ramp-up time by roughly 25%. I trained 5+ new peer counselors.
In a wellness program: Training and supporting wellness champions, managers and staff at every level.
03 · Partners & budgets
I coordinated care plans across clinicians, case managers and county teams, worked with outside partners, helped plan program budgets, and fed program data into grant proposals.
In a wellness program: Getting HR, leaders, physicians and vendors behind one program, inside a budget.
04 · Measuring what works
I gathered feedback through surveys and interviews, wrote data reports for executives, and fixed workflow gaps with leadership. Drop-in participation rose roughly 20% and case resolution got roughly 50% faster.
In a wellness program: Participation data and a dashboard that tells leadership what to keep and what to cut.

Percentages are my own estimates from before-and-after observation.
