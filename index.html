Hi, I'm Susan

I build environments where people can find their own calm.

I ran crisis support for youth and families in San Francisco for five years. Deciding what someone needed, then getting it to them — a phone call, a form, five minutes at the door. Then I taught myself to build software, to ask that same question outside a crisis room: does this hand someone control over their day, or take it from them.

How this translates Get in touch
01

Give people control. Don't take it from them.

Whether it's a product or a daily practice, I'm watching for the same thing: does someone have control over the moments of their day, or is the system making that call instead. I'm not picky about title — content, research, trust & safety, product. I care about being close enough to the people using the thing to know if it's actually working.

I'm most useful to a team building something that hands people back a sense of control over their own day.

02

How peer mentor work becomes product instinct.

What this looks like in practice:

01 · Safety escalation
I triaged crisis cases daily, coordinating clinical, legal, and county teams.
In a product: Deciding whether a report goes to a human now or waits is the same call, made daily.
02 · Consent & privacy
I explained privacy limits to people who agreed without fully understanding them.
In a product: A user tapping "I agree" without reading is that same gap — closing it is work I've done face to face.
03 · Pressured, not persuaded
Trauma-informed care meant never using shame or fear to move someone.
In a product: A buried cancel button or guilt-trip pop-up crosses that same line.

5+ YRS · SF

Lead Peer Mentor, trauma-informed care

Ran the floor of an SF youth drop-in center: food, hygiene, homework help, daily safety calls — roughly 300 youth over five years, 10+ person team. Grew outreach 200%.

When in-person services shut down, I moved the center to a fully virtual format — working out which parts of walking through the door actually mattered and rebuilding those in tools that weren't built for it, until I knew the platforms well enough to make real decisions instead of guessing. People kept showing up.

I build to understand products from the inside.

I'm not a fast learner. I take my time with a new tool before it clicks. Once it does, I go further with it than people who picked it up quicker. These are that pattern, not an exception to it — all on GitHub.

01

Cozy

On the App Store

Chore management without the guilt trip most chore apps run on.

The decision that shaped it: an empty day is a good day. Finish everything and Cozy says "nothing due today — a quiet morning, enjoy it," instead of pushing you toward the next task. The chore library suggests by room and lets you take only what you want.

Roam

On the App Store

Wellness resources across the Bay Area, for someone who doesn't know where to start.

Every listing shows cost before you tap it — free, donation, sliding scale. Nobody finds out something is out of reach after getting their hopes up. Suggestions follow your check-in, so a low day surfaces smaller asks.
