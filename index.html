The Translation

Here's exactly how five years in mental health became product and AI work.

Not a metaphor. The actual mechanics of the job carried over, one for one.

In mental health

For five-plus years I was a lead peer mentor, talking with youth and families every day about what was and wasn't working for them, with real stakes attached to getting it right.

↓ In product & AI

User research, done live. I don't need a research plan to get real signal out of a hard conversation, or to tell you what a person actually needs versus what they said in the interview.

In mental health

I sat between families, clinicians, case managers, and schools. Everyone spoke a different language, and my job was making sure they understood each other well enough to act.

↓ In product & AI

Cross-functional translation. The same job as sitting between users and a product team, or between what a model actually does and what a person experiences when they use it.

In mental health

I wrote instructions and safety plans for people in enrollment, in crisis, in the hardest moments of their week. That writing had to work correctly the first time.

↓ In product & AI

Product and UX writing for people who are stressed, confused, or in a hurry. Onboarding, error states, the exact moments where clarity isn't optional.

In mental health

I watched where care systems failed the people who most needed them to work, and had to notice it fast enough to intervene.

↓ In product & AI

AI product judgment. I use Claude and other AI tools daily, and I know where they help and where they quietly fail the people most likely to lean on them, especially in mental health.
