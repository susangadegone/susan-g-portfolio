02

I've seen what happens when a product doesn't have someone's back.

Several of the young adults I mentored went on to try online dating, and I watched, up close, what happens when the product isn't built to protect them: a catfish that took months to untangle, a match running a scam, someone talked into sending money before they'd ever met in real life. None of that gets fixed with better microcopy — it's a trust and safety problem, exactly the kind of thing five years of peer mentor work trained me to see coming, not clean up after. Online dating asks people to be adventurous with strangers, on purpose. That adventure only holds up if what someone shares along the way is protected by people who know what unsafe actually looks like in someone's life, before launch.

If your product asks people to trust a stranger, let's talk.

03

How peer mentor work becomes product instinct.

Not hypotheticals. This is what I'd actually be doing on your team.

01 · Safety escalation
Decided when a safety concern had to go up the chain and when it didn't, knowing a wrong call in either direction could hurt someone.
At your company: I'd write the escalation rule into the moderation queue itself — what waits for the next review cycle, and what interrupts someone's shift right now.
02 · Consent
Explained confidentiality and its limits to scared teenagers before they'd tell me anything.
At your company: I'd rewrite your consent screen so the disclosure sits where someone actually reads it, not buried above a checkbox — informed consent, not a click-through.
03 · Dark patterns
Practiced trauma-informed care, where you never use shame or fear as a lever.
At your company: I'd flag the growth mechanic in design review before it ships, not after the support tickets pile up — I can tell a nudge from a dark pattern in a Figma file.

If your team needs this, let's talk.
