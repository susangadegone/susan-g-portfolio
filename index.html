01

Give people control. Don't take it from them.

Whether it's a program or a product, I care about the same thing: does someone have control over the big and small moments of their day, or is the system making that call for them.

Most wellness programs get participation by pressure. A challenge board, a leaderboard, a gift card, a nudge that's really a nag. That works for about three weeks. What holds is when showing up is the easiest thing on someone's list.

I'd rather remove five reasons not to come than add one reason to.

I'm most useful to a team that wants a program people keep choosing, not one that has to be sold internally every quarter.

02

What running a floor teaches you about running a program.

What this looks like in practice:

01 · Escalation
I triaged crisis cases daily, working across the clinicians, the county intake team, and legal, sometimes inside the same hour.
In a program: Knowing whether a member needs an app, a person, or a clinician now is the same call. Most wellness stacks get it wrong by defaulting to the app.
02 · Consent & privacy
I explained privacy limits to people who agreed without fully understanding them.
In a program: Someone tapping "I agree" on a health platform is that same gap. If people don't trust where their data goes, your participation numbers are fiction.
03 · Pressured, not persuaded
Trauma-informed care meant never using shame or fear to move someone.
In a program: Step challenges, leaderboards, and a manager forwarding the sign-up link a third time cross that same line. They move the number this quarter and cost you the next one.
03

Programs I've run, and what each one taught me.

The problem, what I did about it, and what happened.

01 · Service redesign

Rebuilding a drop-in center that couldn't have a door.

PROBLEM
The center's whole value was physical. Food, hygiene, homework help, someone noticing your face when you walked in. In-person services shut down and none of that had a remote version.
WHAT I DID
Broke down what walking through the door actually gave someone, piece by piece, and rebuilt each piece in tools that weren't built for it. Learned those platforms well enough to make real calls instead of guessing at them.
RESULT
People kept showing up.

This is the same problem every digital health program has: the thing that works in a room, delivered through a screen.

02 · Outreach & participation

Participation up 200%, without a single incentive.

PROBLEM
Mid-pandemic, the young people who needed the center most had no way to find it, and no reason to believe it was still open.
WHAT I DID
Built an outreach program from scratch and started the center's first Instagram in 2022, now around 550 followers. Went to where people already were instead of waiting for them to come to us.
RESULT
Participant count up 200%.

No gift cards, no challenge board. Being findable, and being worth finding.

03 · Testing the instinct

Building three wellness apps to check whether I was right.

PROBLEM
I believed that giving people control is what makes them come back. I didn't know whether that held up outside a crisis room, or whether it was just something I'd decided was true.
WHAT I DID
Built Cozy, Roam, and a calendar mood tracker from scratch. Ran interviews for each one, then changed features based on what people actually did, not what they said they'd do.
RESULT
Won the 2024 Neolth Innovation Challenge, 1st of 11 teams. Picked for Stanford's AI for Good cohort, 230 applicants down to 30, then top 8 to pitch investors.

These are prototypes on TestFlight, not shipped products with a user base. I built them to learn, and they did their job.
