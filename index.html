What I Bring

Five years in trauma-informed care taught me things a product background doesn't.

i. I know users the hard way.

For five-plus years I was a lead peer mentor working with youth and families in trauma-informed care. I talked with people every day about what was and wasn't working for them. That's user research, done live, with real stakes.

ii. I translate.

In that work I sat between families, clinicians, case managers, and schools. Everyone spoke a different language. My job was making sure they understood each other. That's the same job as sitting between users and a product team.

iii. I write for people in hard moments.

Enrollment, onboarding, asking for help. I know how to write so a stressed person can actually follow it.

iv. I work with AI every day.

I use Claude and other AI tools to research, build, and write. I know what these systems do well and where they fail people, especially in mental health, where the people most likely to lean on AI are the ones most hurt when it gets things wrong.
