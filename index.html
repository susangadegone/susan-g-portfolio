Hi, I'm Susan

Product work, sharpened by real trust, safety, and privacy instinct.

I came to product to help people at scale instead of one at a time. Before that, for over five years, I sat across from teenagers in the worst moment of their week and had to decide, in real time, what was safe to say and what wasn't. I'm skeptical of AI. I also think it can help. I've stopped trying to make those two positions shake hands.

How this translates Get in touch
01

I know your user before they open the app.

Five years as a trauma-informed peer mentor taught me things no product course will. I learned to read a room for danger before anyone said a word. I learned the difference between a teenager who actually understood what confidentiality meant and one who just nodded because that's what you do when an adult asks you something. Both are judgment calls made under pressure, with someone's actual safety riding on getting it right. I built three wellness apps after that, to see whether the same judgment held up once it was software instead of a conversation. Now when I look at a product, the question in my head isn't whether it's clever. It's who the product fails first, and what that costs them.

If you're building something that helps people get care they wouldn't otherwise have access to, let's talk.

02

I've seen what happens when a product doesn't have someone's back.

Several of the young adults I mentored started online dating. I watched, closer than I wanted to, what happens when the product wasn't built to protect them. One spent months untangling a catfish. Another sent money to someone he'd never met, talked into it slowly enough that it didn't feel like a mistake until it already was. You don't fix that with better microcopy. Five years of peer mentor work trained me to see a trust and safety problem coming, not clean up after one. Online dating asks people to be adventurous with strangers on purpose. That only works if whoever built the product understood what unsafe actually looks like in someone's life before it shipped, not after someone reports it.

If your product asks people to trust a stranger, let's talk.

03

How peer mentor work becomes product instinct.

Not hypotheticals. This is what I'd actually be doing on your team.

01 · Safety escalation
Decided when a safety concern had to go up the chain and when it didn't, knowing a wrong call in either direction could hurt someone.
At your company: I'd build the escalation rule into the moderation queue itself, not a policy doc nobody opens under pressure. Some flags wait. Some interrupt someone's shift immediately. The system has to already know the difference.
02 · Consent & privacy
Explained confidentiality and its limits to scared teenagers before they'd tell me anything.
At your company: I'd move your consent screen's real disclosure out from under the checkbox, somewhere a person would actually read it before clicking through. I'd hold your privacy policy to the same bar. Agreement isn't the same as understanding, and I can tell the difference in how a screen is built.
03 · Dark patterns
Practiced trauma-informed care, where you never use shame or fear as a lever.
At your company: I'd flag the growth mechanic in design review, before it ships, not after support tickets start piling up. I can tell a nudge from a dark pattern by looking at a Figma file, the same way I could tell a real question from a leading one across a mentoring table.

If your team needs this, let's talk.
